Out of Time

By: Ariel Fajardo - 10 minute read -

The humming machinery of the production factory ran day and night, a tireless backdrop to the lives inside. Johnathan worked in the harsh, buzzing lights of the baggage counter as an attendant, tied to a strict five-month contract that required him to lift heavy bags for hours on end. Janice, on the other hand, worked alongside at the back of the canteen, in the quiet, air-conditioned sanctuary of the accounting office, keeping track of the factory’s logistics and payroll. It was during his third month on the job when Johnathan first saw her. She was a new employee, standing at his counter to deposit her bag. For Johnathan, it was love at first sight. To catch her attention, Johnathan asked "What is your job here?" Janice then replied "I am an accountant in here." That was the first interaction between Johnathan and Janice.

For weeks, his mind raced with ideas on how to catch her attention again. Desperate for a connection, he finally looked through the deposit logbook, tracing the rows until he found the column where she had written her name: Janice.

Armed with her name and weeks of careful planning, an idea took shape. Because he was a baggage attendant, he had direct access to the baggage counter racks. He located her handbag by its tag number and, when the counter was quiet, he carefully slipped a small, handwritten note into the side pocket. It read: "Is your name Janice? Can I add you on Facebook and sketch your face? By the way, my name is Johnathan."

Later that afternoon, Janice came down for her break to claim her bag. Johnathan’s hands trembled with nerves as he handed it over. She walked to the canteen, sat down, and opened her pocket to find the folded slip of paper. When her break ended, she returned to the counter to check her bag back in. She didn't say a word. She didn’t look at him, and she never wrote back. Despite the silence, Johnathan’s hope didn't fade. He went ahead and drew her portrait anyway.

The next day, he carefully slid the finished sketch into her bag. A day later, after lunch, the quiet routine was broken. Two of Janice’s coworkers approached the counter, giggling, and asked Johnathan what his name was, if he was single, and if he had a girlfriend. Behind them, Janice with her friends alongside Maan stood frozen in the front of corridor of baggage counter, watching silently. Overwhelmed by nerves, Johnathan couldn't even speak; he merely gestured with his hands that he was single and had no girlfriend. He quickly scribbled his name on a piece of paper and handed it to the coworkers. Confirming his identity, the girls began to loudly tease Janice. "Yieeeee! Hey Janice, he’s single! Hahaha!" Their laughter echoed down the hallway as they turned the corner, leaving Johnathan with a racing heart.

Days passed, when Janice punched out of work one evening to claim her bag, Johnathan decided to take a bolder step. He had spent hours braiding a handmade paracord bracelet. Extending his shaking hand, he said softly, "I just made it for you." To his absolute delight, Janice smiled warmly. "Thank you," she replied. As she walked away, Johnathan could have jumped for joy. Not only had she smiled and spoken to him, but their fingers had briefly brushed when he handed her the bracelet. He was completely thrilled. As the days turned into weeks, Johnathan still wonders why Janice didn't reply to the messages he sent on Facebook. Every time she walked down the corridor or handed him her bag, his pulse skyrocketed. Yet, he still had no idea how she truly felt. Hoping to recreate the magic of her smile, he resorted to his quiet routine again. He began slipping shorter, gentler notes into her bag: "Hello Janice, how are you?" and "Hello Janice, have a great day." But the silence returned, heavier this time. There were no replies. She still hadn't accepted his Facebook friend and message request.

As the weeks ticked away, the quiet grew suffocating. Johnathan was left completely in the dark, comforting himself with the thought that she was probably just too shy to show her feelings and keeps thinking that maybe Janice does not like him. Meanwhile, the clock was ticking. He only had two months left on his contract, and the end was fast approaching. Johnathan still didn't give up.

Soon, another two weeks passed with no response. Then, one evening, fate seemed to give him a real chance. Johnathan and Janice punched out of the factory at the exact same time. Johnathan punched out of work first, went to the locker, changed his clothes, and walked toward the exit gate of factory, and he arrived at the main road then waits for a jeep. While Johnathan sitting on the curb, waiting for a jeep, after about 10 minutes, Janice with her friend Maan punched out of work, exited the factory gate, walks toward the main road and they arrived at the jeep stop too. Johnathan then saw Janice at a crowd of commuters waiting under the dim streetlights.

Seizing the moment, Johnathan stepped up next to Janice, who was standing with her friend and coworker, Maan. "Where do you go home to?" Johnathan asked, his voice trembling. Janice instantly froze. A wave of visible discomfort washed over her. She glued her eyes to her phone screen, refusing to look up or acknowledge him. Sensing the heavy tension, Maan quickly stepped in to shield her friend. "We live in Asilang," Maan replied politely.

When a crowded jeepney finally pulled up, they all hopped inside, squeezed tightly among the other passengers. At a major crossroads, the jeepney halted. Johnathan and Janice both stepped out onto the pavement to catch their connecting ride. Maan stayed on, waving through the window as the jeepney rolled away into the night. Suddenly, it was just the two of them, walking alone toward the next bus stop. Desperate to bridge the distance, Johnathan tried again. "You live in Asilang too? I live in Asilang too." "Yes," Janice replied.

Her voice was ice-cold. Keeping her eyes locked onto her phone, her posture incredibly tense, she deliberately took a sharp step away from him and accelerated her pace, putting physical distance between them. Johnathan’s mind raced in confusion. What went wrong? he wondered desperately. Did I do something? Or is she just uncomfortable because it's the middle of the night and we're alone? When they reached the bus stop, Johnathan remained standing, while Janice walked several paces away, sitting down on the concrete curb. She kept her head down, texting furiously.

When the bus finally arrived, they boarded together, but the atmosphere between them was thick and suffocating. Janice sat on the right side with someone at the front of the bus while Johnathan sat at about three rows away from her at the left side. He was alone staring outside the window into the darkness. When the bus reached her neighborhood, she got off without a single backward glance. Johnathan glanced at Janice going out of the bus then he stared again out the window into the darkness until his own stop appeared a few miles later.

Before he knew it, Johnathan was facing his final three days at the factory. Two days before his departure, Janice came to the counter at the end of her shift. Johnathan handed her a box of chocolates with a small note taped to the top: "Hello Janice, this is for you. Have a nice day." Surprised, she took them and quietly thanked him. Johnathan simply smiled back, a bittersweet feeling washing over him as he realized his time was up. The day before his contract ended, he sat down and poured his heart into a poignant goodbye letter, with a cross pendant, and quietly tucking it into her bag.

On his absolute final day, he slipped one last, short farewell note and a cross pendant into her pocket. When Janice’s shift ended for the very last time, she walked up to the counter and handed him her number tag. Johnathan retrieved the bag from the rack and passed it to her. Janice took it silently, offering no words, no answers, and no goodbye. Johnathan stood at the baggage counter window and watched her leave.

He looked out as she walked down the long corridor with Maan, slinging her bag over her shoulder. He watched her figure grow smaller and smaller until she turned the corner at the exit and completely disappeared. That was the last time Johnathan saw Janice. His last shift ended few hours later and his contract expired. A week later, Johnathan returned to factory for clearance. Johnathan hoped to see Janice again, but to no avail, he didn't see her again.

Then 4 months later, Johnathan returned to the factory as a Production Worker. Johnathan found out that Janice no longer worked there, having either finished her contract or resigned. Two weeks later, Johnathan did not last long as a Production Worker, he goes AWOL (Absent Without Leave) and he vanished from the factory like a ghost, and leaving no trace behind.

A year has passed since that final day. Johnathan still thinks about Janice every single day, often wondering why she left that Facebook request pending forever.

In the quiet moments of the evening, the old questions still echo in his mind—did she genuinely dislike him, or was she just too shy to speak up? But as the night deepens, the thoughts drift away. He gently reminds himself that it doesn't matter anymore.

The story is finished, and the characters have moved on. The contract was over, he had left the factory like a ghost, no longer lived in Asilang, and he already said his final goodbye.

Johnathan finally realizes Janice's discomfort means she doesn't like him and he never stood a chance with Janice.

Johnathan accepted that he will be single forever and now lives in shrouded mystery.

I Hope you like this short unrequited love story.
